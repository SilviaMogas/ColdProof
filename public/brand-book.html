Brand overview

ColdProof is not a medical app. It is a trust infrastructure layer for temperature-sensitive supply chains.

PAGE 03

Brand foundations

What the company is for, in four statements and four values.

Layout
Mission and vision as full-width statements in display type, stacked, divided by a hairline. Values below as a four-column row.
Design instructions
No imagery on this page at all. If it needs a picture to hold attention, the statements are not written well enough. Values get one sentence each — never a paragraph.
MissionVerify every temperature-sensitive delivery from origin to destination.
VisionA world where medicine movement is transparent, accountable and condition verified.

Values

01

Evidence

We show the reading before we draw the conclusion. Nothing is asserted that was not measured.

02

Precision

A range, a timestamp and a checkpoint ID. Vague language is a failure of the product, not of the copy.

03

Accountability

A breach belongs to a leg of the chain, not to the shipment as a whole. Naming it is the point.

04

Trust

Earned by a record no single party can edit, not claimed by a word on a website.

Voice

ColdProof speaks like infrastructure. Not pharma advertising, not crypto marketing, not startup hype.

Do
  • State measurements. "Held between 2 and 8 °C across 14 checkpoints."
  • State consequences. "Payment released on verified delivery."
  • Use operational language. Excursion, checkpoint, custody, threshold, batch.
  • Explain evidence. "Breach at CP-07. Batch held."
Avoid
  • Hype. Revolutionising healthcare. Unlocking the future. Disrupting logistics. Next-generation solution.
  • Fear marketing. Never lead with harm to patients to make a commercial point.
  • Overclaiming. Not "we guarantee medicine safety" — "we verify conditions."
  • Blockchain-first framing. The ledger is how it works, not what it sells.
PAGE 04

Logo system

A six-arm crystal verification symbol. The inner void holds the check. Nothing is stacked, contained or added.

Layout
Primary mark on white and on ink, side by side at equal size. Below: three lockup variants in a row, then the construction table, then the size ladder, then the misuse row.
Design instructions
Every rule gets a rendered example beside it. All logo panels share identical dimensions so the variants read as one system rather than a menu of options.
Icon only

App icon, favicon, box print, checkpoint stamp.

Wordmark only
ColdProof

Footers and partner walls, where the icon already appears.

Horizontal lockup
ColdProof

The default. Navigation, slide corners, packaging.

ConstructionOn a 96-unit grid, centre at 48,48. One arm runs from radius 20 to 42, carrying two pairs of barbs at 30% and 68% of its length, each at 60° to the arm. That arm is rotated six times by 60°. Perfect radial symmetry — no arm is ever shortened or removed.
The inner voidRadius 20, so the arms' round caps stop at 17.25 from centre. The check sits entirely inside that void, reaching 12.6 at its furthest point including stroke. That leaves a clear gap of 4.6 units on every side.
Separation ruleThe cyan check must never touch, overlap or visually crowd a white or ink arm. If the gap drops below 4 units at any point, the check is too large or off centre. This is the single most common way to break the mark.
Check geometryVertex at 44.8, 53.8. Short arm to 39.7, 47.8. Long arm to 56.3, 42.3. The long arm is roughly 1.9× the short one — below 1.6 it stops reading as a check and starts reading as a chevron.
StrokeArms and barbs 5.5 units. Check 5 units. Round caps and joins throughout.
Lockup spacingGap between icon and wordmark equals one arm length at icon scale. Wordmark cap height equals 0.62 of icon height.
Clear spaceOne arm length (36 units) on all four sides. Nothing enters it — no type, no rules, no image edges.
Minimum sizeFull mark from 32 px. Below that, the simplified mark: barbs removed, arms at 8 units, check reduced to a solid safe cold core of radius 12 — which keeps the same 4-unit gap to the arms.
ColourInk, white or safe cold only. The mark never carries breach amber, and never changes colour to report a state.

Size behaviour

Distort, stretch or squash.

Colour system

Four colours and three supports. Each one reports something. None of them decorate.

Layout
Four large swatches across the top with meaning beneath each. Support neutrals as a smaller row. Ratio bar full width below, then usage rules as a table, then two worked examples.
Design instructions
Swatch height 116 px minimum so the colour reads as a surface rather than a chip. Hex values in mono, always. The ratio bar is literal — its segment widths match the stated percentages.

Ink

#0A1B2E

Structure, typography, brand foundation. The default for almost everything.

Safe cold

#0FB5D6

Verified state, in-range temperature, successful settlement. The only verification colour.

Breach

Temperature excursion, hold state, payment withheld. Use rarely.

Frost

#EDF2F6

Backgrounds, surfaces, inactive states.

Support

Slate

#5B6E80

Secondary text, eyebrows, captions.

Lines

#D6E0E8

Hairlines, table rules, dividers.

Paper

#FBFCFD

Page ground.

Ratio

70% INK + FROST
20% WHITE
8%

70% ink and frost · 20% white · 8% safe cold · 2% breach

Green is retiredGreen never appears anywhere in the system — not as success, not as a chart series, not as an icon accent. Safe cold is the only verification colour. This is what keeps ColdProof from looking like a generic dashboard.
Breach disciplineBreach amber is earned. It appears when a condition has failed and nowhere else. An amber heading, an amber button or an amber divider is a misuse.
Ink tintsFor the shipment box and other dimensional artwork, ink may step to #12253C and #1C3350. These are tonal steps of ink, not new colours, and they never carry meaning.
ContrastNever set safe cold type on white below 18 px. Never set ink type on safe cold as a large field. Tinted backgrounds take their own dark values: #075E70 on cyan tint, #7A4405 on amber tint.
Never colour aloneA verified checkpoint carries a tick glyph, a breach carries a cross. State must survive a bad projector and colour-blind viewers.

Why it mattersThe mono face is what makes a slide read as a record rather than a pitch. If a number appears in the body face, the page loses the single strongest signal the brand has after the mark.

Design instructions
The box is flat axonometric, three faces, built from ink tints with safe cold detailing. Never a photorealistic render and never a photograph of a cooler. Components sit on frost, never on white, so they read as a kit.

PAGE 10

Presentation principles

Six rules that carry the brand onto a stage, where slides are read from six metres in about eight seconds each.

Layout
Six principle cards in a two-column grid, then a slide sequence table.
Design instructions
Each principle gets a verb-first title and one sentence. Anything longer belongs in this book, not on a stage.
01

One idea per slide

A display headline and at most one supporting line. If a slide needs a paragraph, it is two slides.

02

Numbers in mono

Every temperature, timestamp and checkpoint ID in JetBrains Mono. This is what makes the deck read as measured rather than pitched.

03

No 3D renders

Flat axonometric artwork or a real photograph. Rendered props read as filler to a technical jury.

04

Use the temperature band

The 2–8 trace is the transition between acts. It turns amber the moment the failure case is introduced.

05

Show the failure clearly

A system that can say no is more convincing than one that only says yes. Let the amber land without narration.

06

Open with the claim

Slide one is the primary line on frost with the mark. No agenda slide, no team slide before the problem.

What to cutThe agenda slide, the market-size slide with an unsourced figure, the architecture diagram, and any slide built from three identical rounded cards.
SourcingAny statistic on a slide carries a named primary source and a year. An unsourced number is the fastest way to lose a technical jury.
TimingSlides 01–03 in forty seconds. Slides 04–08 in two minutes. Demo two minutes. Close in twenty seconds.
APPENDIX A

Generation prompts

Paste-ready prompts for image and slide generation. Each one carries the palette, the constraints and the prohibitions, so an off-brand result is a prompt failure rather than a taste dispute.

Layout
Prompt blocks on ink, monospaced, each with a title above and a usage note below.
Design instructions
Never remove the prohibition list from a prompt. It is doing most of the work — image models default to gradients, glows and 3D unless told otherwise.

A1 · Shipment box, hero asset

Flat vector illustration of an insulated medical shipment box in axonometric projection, three visible faces. Palette strictly: #0A1B2E, #12253C, #1C3350 for the three faces, #0FB5D6 for accents only. Details: a six-arm snowflake outline on the left face in #0FB5D6; a small rectangular digital readout on the right face showing "2-8°C" in a monospace face in #0FB5D6; a two-segment status strip beneath the readout. Style: clean geometric vector, flat fills, 2px dark outline, no texture. Background: transparent or #FBFCFD. Do not include: gradients, drop shadows, glow, reflections, 3D rendering, photorealism, ice crystals, snow, human hands, medical crosses, green, red, brand logos, any text other than the readout.

Use for: cover, page 07, deck slide 04. Regenerate at 2000 px minimum for print.

A2 · Checkpoint route diagram

Flat vector diagram of a horizontal supply chain route, left to right, on a #FBFCFD background. Six nodes as filled circles connected by a thin #D6E0E8 line: origin, four checkpoints, destination. Origin and destination filled #0A1B2E. Checkpoints filled #0FB5D6 with a #0A1B2E tick glyph inside, except the second checkpoint, filled #F08C1E with a #0A1B2E cross glyph. Under each node, two lines of small uppercase monospace label in #5B6E80. Style: schematic, precise, generous whitespace, hairline connectors. Do not include: gradients, shadows, glow, 3D, isometric perspective, icons of trucks or planes, green, red, decorative flourishes.

Use for: page 08, deck slides 05 to 07. Generate the compliant and breach versions separately and keep them structurally identical.

A3 · Temperature band, section device

Flat vector line chart, very wide and short, aspect ratio roughly 16:1. Two horizontal hairlines in #D6E0E8 marking upper and lower thresholds. A single polyline temperature trace between them in #0FB5D6, 2.5px, rounded joins, gently irregular. Optional breach variant: the trace crosses above the upper threshold at around 55% of the width, turns #F08C1E for that segment only, then returns to #0FB5D6 on recovery. Background transparent. Do not include: axis labels, gridlines, data points, fill under the curve, gradients, shadows, glow, any text.

Use for: section dividers throughout the book, and as the transition between acts in the deck.

A4 · Title slide

Presentation slide, 16:9, background #EDF2F6. Headline in Space Grotesk Bold, tracking -3%, colour #0A1B2E, sentence case, left aligned, occupying the left two thirds: "Proof of delivery is not proof of safe delivery." The single word "safe" in #0FB5D6. Small uppercase monospace eyebrow above the headline in #5B6E80. A six-arm snowflake mark with a small check in its centre, #0A1B2E, bottom-left at 24px, next to the wordmark ColdProof. Generous margins, no other elements. Do not include: gradients, photographs, stock imagery, icons, decorative shapes, green, red, centred logos.

Use for: deck slides 01 and 10. The same template with the eyebrow changed carries slides 02, 03 and 08.

A5 · Dashboard mockup

Flat UI mockup of an operations dashboard, light theme on #FBFCFD, single card with a 1px #D6E0E8 border. Dark header bar in #0A1B2E with small white uppercase monospace title. Four table rows, each with a monospace shipment ID and product name on the left in #5B6E80, and a status chip on the right. Chips: three in a pale cyan tint with dark cyan text reading "Verified", one in a pale amber tint with dark amber text reading "Breach · CP-02". Style: clean enterprise SaaS, hairline rules, generous row padding, no icons. Do not include: gradients, shadows, rounded card corners above 4px, sidebars, avatars, charts, green, red, dark mode.

Use for: page 09 and deck slide 09. Keep it schematic — a mockup that looks shipped invites questions about a product that is not.

A6 · Iconography set

Set of six monoline vector icons on a 24 unit grid, 2px stroke, round caps, no fills, colour #0A1B2E, transparent background, arranged in a single row with equal spacing. Icons: a six-arm snowflake; a shield; a check mark; a thermometer; a wallet; a shipping box in flat front view. Style: consistent stroke weight across all six, geometric construction, optically balanced, no containers or circles behind the icons. Do not include: fills, gradients, shadows, perspective, colour, drop caps, labels.

Use for: the icon library. The shield belongs to this set only and never appears behind or around the logo.

One rule for every prompt. If the output contains green, a gradient, a glow or a 3D render, it is off brand regardless of how good it looks. Regenerate rather than adjust.
